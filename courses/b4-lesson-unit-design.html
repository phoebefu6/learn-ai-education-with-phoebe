Part 1 · covers Vanderbilt course-design guidance + Khanmigo teacher features

Backward design with an AI co-designer 7 min live

Start where the unit ends: what will students DO to show they learned it? Name that evidence first. Then choose activities that build toward it, then materials that support the activities - never the reverse order, and never materials first just because AI makes materials cheap. AI drafts at every stage: Khanmigo's teacher tools generate learning objectives, rubrics, and exit tickets, and Vanderbilt's course-design guidance walks faculty through using AI for course objectives, activities, and assessment ideas. But the order of operations, the objectives, and the bar stay with the teacher. AI is the fastest first-draft colleague you have ever had - it is not the teacher of record.

LiveWho drafts, who owns - stage by stage5 min▶

The practitioner pattern, at every stage of the flow above:

  • Objectives - AI drafts eight candidate objectives from your topic and your named evidence. You prune to three or four and rewrite them in your students' language. Pruning IS the design act - what you cut defines the unit as much as what you keep.
  • Rubrics - AI drafts the rows; you rewrite the top band yourself, because the top band is your standard. A rubric whose top band you did not write is a bar you did not set.
  • Exit tickets - AI generates them in seconds per objective per week; you check each one actually tests the objective and not vocabulary recall.
  • Materials - the safest delegation of all (b1's "delegate freely" bucket): leveled readings, worked examples, sentence starters - always teacher-reviewed before students see them.
Real world

This split is already productized. Khanmigo's teacher tools generate learning objectives, rubrics, and exit tickets - and it is free for educators 18+ in 44+ countries (Microsoft-funded). On the higher-ed side, Vanderbilt's teaching-center guidance walks faculty through AI for course objectives, activities, and assessment ideas. Both put the same worker in the same seat: AI drafts, the educator decides.

LiveThe 15-minute single-lesson version3 min▶

Most weeks you are not designing a unit - you are designing Tuesday. Same order of operations, one class period:

  • Name the exit evidence - one thing students do before the bell that shows the lesson landed. One sentence, written by you, before AI opens.
  • AI drafts the middle - a hook, one main activity, and the exit ticket, all pointed at that evidence.
  • Prune and fit-check - cut what does not build the evidence, adjust for the class you actually have on a Tuesday afternoon.

Fifteen minutes, most of it yours. The unit build in Part 2 is exactly this skill at four-week scale - which is why we practice the small version first.

Self-studyUnit design through the 4D loop3 min read▶

Run tonight's work back through b1's loop and it holds up cleanly:

  • Delegation - drafting objectives, activities, and materials is "delegate freely with review". Setting the objectives and the top rubric band is "do not delegate" - that judgment is the job.
  • Description - the unit brief you will meet in Part 2 is Description done properly: class size, levels, topic, fixed evidence, constraints.
  • Discernment - pruning eight AI objectives to four, catching the exit ticket that tests vocabulary instead of argument.
  • Diligence - the Dana rule below: differentiated materials described by need, never by student record.
Part 2 · covers ADGIE (Hardman) + the build-along

ADGIE, then build Riverview unit v1 24 min live

If you design learning for a living - instructional designer, L&D, curriculum lead - Philippa Hardman's ADGIE is the classic ADDIE cycle with AI written into every phase. One honesty note before the table: ADGIE is our frame for instructional design with AI - the objectives-and-rubrics pattern in Part 1 stands on Khanmigo's teacher features and Vanderbilt's guidance, not on ADGIE, which does not cover them.

ADGIE stageWhat AI doesWhat the designer does
AnalysisBuilds learner personas and skill maps from interview dataRuns the interviews, validates the personas against real learners
DesignProposes multiple pedagogical prototypes to comparePicks the pedagogy and the trade-offs - the taste decision
GenerationFirst-drafts text, scripts, and knowledge checksQuality-controls every draft - the designer QCs, always
IndividualisationAdapts content to the learner in real timeSets the bounds of adaptation and watches for drift
EvaluationRuns continuously instead of as a final stepDecides what the signals mean and what changes
Self-studyADGIE or backward design - which frame when2 min read▶

They are not competitors; they are different zoom levels.

  • Backward design - one teacher, one unit, one class. Evidence-first thinking you can run at your desk tonight, which is why the build-along uses it.
  • ADGIE - design at scale: full courses, curricula, corporate modules. The Analysis and continuous Evaluation stages only earn their keep when there are many learners and many iterations - b9 runs ADGIE end to end on a corporate onboarding module.

Both share the guardrail that matters: AI drafts, the human owns the pedagogy and the bar. If you remember one thing from the two frames, remember the shared rule.

LiveBuild-along: Riverview unit v1, in four moves18 min · type along▶

First, the prompt that wastes the session versus the one that builds the unit. This is b2's lesson wearing its unit-design costume:

The generic ask - what most teachers typeMake me a lesson plan about persuasive essays for Grade 9.
The context-rich unit brief - paste, then buildYou are co-designing a 4-week Grade 9 persuasive-essay unit with me. I am the teacher of record: I own the objectives, and I will prune everything you draft. Context: Riverview Secondary, 28 students, mixed writing levels, several non-native English speakers. Topic: "Should our city ban single-use plastics?" Evidence of learning (fixed - do not change it): students plan, draft, and defend a persuasive essay - claim, evidence, counterargument - and can explain their argument aloud. Draft one section at a time and wait for my edits before continuing: 1. Eight candidate unit objectives aligned to that evidence, in student-friendly language. I will prune to four. 2. A 4-week arc: week-by-week activities that build toward the evidence. 3. A differentiated materials set for a Grade 9 student who struggles with written organization - sentence starters, paragraph frames, one worked example on a DIFFERENT topic. Do not ask for or use any real student's name or records. 4. One exit ticket per week - three questions each, checking that week's objective. Constraints: activities must produce visible student thinking - outlines, drafts, spoken reasons - not just polished text.

Objectives. Paste the brief. AI drafts eight; prune to four live, saying out loud why each cut goes. Ms. Tan keeps "build a counterargument from a source you disagree with" and cuts two that were really activities in disguise.

The 4-week arc. Week 1 claims and evidence, week 2 counterarguments, week 3 drafting, week 4 revision and speaking. Check the arc against the evidence, not against coverage.

Differentiated materials. The brief describes "a Grade 9 student who struggles with written organization" - that is Dana's need with zero of Dana's data. Same sentence starters, no disclosure.

Exit tickets. Four weeks, three questions each. Discernment pass: week 2's first draft tested plastic-recycling facts, not counterargument skill. Regenerate with the objective quoted.

The Dana rule - privacy preview for b8 Describe the need, never the student. "A Grade 9 student who struggles with written organization" gets you the same paragraph frames as pasting her IEP into a consumer chatbot - without the unauthorized disclosure. Profiles in, records never. b8 builds the full rails.

Part 3 · self-study

Unit v1 has a hole - and we are leaving it open after class

Look hard at what you built tonight. Objectives: solid. Arc: solid. Materials: differentiated. Exit tickets: weekly. And the assessment? Still one polished final essay. Unit v1 grades exactly the artifact AI produces best - which means b1's 14-of-28 polished-drafts moment happens in THIS unit too, on schedule, around week 4. We name that honestly instead of pretending the design work is done: the fix is a redesigned assessment, and it is the whole of b5.

Self-studyWhy the hole matters more than the polish3 min read▶

A beautifully designed unit with a final-draft-only assessment is a beautifully designed unit that cannot tell you who learned. The objectives say "defend an argument aloud" - but nothing in v1's grading actually collects that evidence. Everything we built tonight points at visible thinking; the assessment still only looks at the finished surface. Hold that tension until b5, where the essay unit gets process portfolios, an in-class component, a 5-minute oral defense, and an AI-transparent rubric - and the hole closes for good.

Self-studyDifferentiation at scale - the quiet superpower3 min read▶

Gallup's 2025 teacher survey puts modifying materials to fit student levels at 28% of teacher AI use - behind preparing to teach (37%) and worksheets (33%), and arguably more valuable than both. Before AI, one teacher hand-building three reading levels of the same text for 28 students was a weekend. Now it is a prompt pattern: fix the content, vary the scaffold - leveled versions, sentence starters, first-language glossaries for your non-native English speakers, extension questions for the fast finishers. The craft point from tonight still applies: describe needs, never students, and review every variant before it ships.

Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 20-30 min total

  • Rewrite the unit brief for one of YOUR units: your class, your levels, your fixed evidence of learning. Run it, and prune the AI's eight objectives down to four - notice what the cutting teaches you.
  • Generate one differentiated materials set using a need profile ("a student who struggles with...") instead of any real student's name or records.
  • Draft exit tickets for your current unit and run one Discernment pass: does each question test the objective, or just recall?
  • Find the hole in your own unit v1: if a student outsourced the final artifact to AI, would your assessment notice? Write the answer in one sentence and bring it to b5.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · You sit down with AI to design the Riverview unit. What does backward design say you name FIRST?

Evidence first, then activities that build it, then materials that support them. AI drafts at every stage - but if you start from materials because they are cheap to generate, you get a unit shaped by what AI produces easily instead of what students need to show.

2 · In Hardman's ADGIE, the Generation stage has AI first-drafting text, scripts, and knowledge checks. Who does quality control?

Generation means first drafts, never final ones. The designer quality-controls every draft - the same split as Part 1's classroom pattern: AI produces volume, the human owns the bar. Learners meeting unreviewed AI content is the failure mode, not the process.

3 · Unit v1 ships tonight with objectives, a 4-week arc, differentiated materials, and exit tickets. What is the hole b5 exists to fix?

v1 grades exactly the artifact AI produces best - a polished final draft - so the 14-of-28 moment from b1 would replay in this unit on schedule. The objectives are fine (you pruned them). The fix is redesigning the assessment around process evidence, which is all of b5.
