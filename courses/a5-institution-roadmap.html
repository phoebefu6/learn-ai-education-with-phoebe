Part 1 · covers UNESCO AI CFT for Teachers + the vendor PD ladder + EDUCAUSE 2025

The PD spine: UNESCO's 15 blocks, climbed on free courses 15 min live

Most institutions run AI PD as a vibe: a keynote, a lunch-and-learn, done. UNESCO's AI Competency Framework for Teachers (2024) replaces the vibe with a grid: 5 dimensions - Human-centred mindset · Ethics of AI · AI foundations and applications · AI pedagogy · AI for professional development - each at 3 levels (Acquire / Deepen / Create), giving 15 blocks you can audit staff against. A parallel student framework exists too - 4 dimensions x 3 levels (Understand / Apply / Create) - and it is your curriculum pointer, not tonight's topic. Your PD budget's job is to move every teacher out of the Acquire row.

Why a rail and not a syllabus The vendor courses each cover a slice of the 15 blocks; none covers all of them. The rail is what stops your PD program being whichever course had the best marketing this term - every rung gets mapped onto the dimensions it actually serves, and the unmapped blocks tell you what to commission internally.

LiveReading the three levels: where the money goes4 min▶

The framework's three levels are progression stages, not grades - and each one prices differently:

  • Acquire - first contact with a dimension: knows the ethics issues exist, has drafted a lesson with AI. Rungs 1-2 of the ladder move a whole staff to Acquire for the cost of two afternoons.
  • Deepen - applies the dimension in their own practice: runs the 4D loop unprompted, adapts tools to their learners, redesigns an assessment. Rungs 3-5 plus real classroom artifacts live here.
  • Create - builds for others: designs PD, drafts policy, mentors colleagues. Champion territory - rung 6, with its 15 hours and grad credit, is the honest price tag.
A 15-minute audit to run this month Hand staff the 5 x 3 grid and ask them to self-place with one line of evidence per dimension. The empty cells ARE your PD plan - and the Create column tells you who your a6 champions are.
Self-studyRunning the ladder as an audit, not a checkbox3 min read▶

Certificates measure attendance; the UNESCO grid measures competence. Two habits turn the ladder into an audit:

  • Map each rung to dimensions. Rung 1 mostly serves AI foundations and applications at Acquire; rung 3 pushes Ethics of AI and AI pedagogy toward Deepen; rung 6 is where Create-level work (designing PD for others) lives. A teacher's transcript of completions becomes a filled-in grid.
  • Ask for one artifact per rung. A redesigned lesson, a policy comment, a rubric draft. The educator track (b1-b10) is built exactly this way - point your staff there for the practitioner depth.
Real world

The strategy gap is measurable. EDUCAUSE 2025 (about 800 higher-ed respondents): 57% call AI a strategic priority, but only 22% have an institution-wide AI strategy, and 39% have acceptable-use policies - up from 23% the year before. The #1 strategy element among those who have one: faculty training, at 63%, with staff training at 56%. Training is not a line item in the strategy. For most institutions it IS the strategy.

Build-vs-buy: student-facing AI with the guardrail test 24 min workshop

Every vendor deck you will see this year sells one of two things: raw access to a frontier model, or a guardrailed-by-design product built on the same model. a2's evidence says these are not two flavors - they are opposite outcomes. Judge everything against the three benchmark products below, because between them they define what "designed for learning" currently looks like.

Bring one real decision - the vendor pitch in your inbox, a teacher's tool request, a board member's enthusiasm - and write it at the top of a page.

Place it against the three benchmarks below: which of Khanmigo, Claude for Education, or ChatGPT for Teachers is it closest to - and is it MORE or LESS guardrailed than that benchmark?

Run the three rubric questions in order: guardrail design, data agreement, under-13 posture. A miss on question one ends the conversation - questions two and three never get asked.

Pick the lane and write it down: buy (passes the rubric) · build (the b6 prompt pattern) · wait (fails today - onto a6's watch list, not the budget).

LiveThe decision rubric: three questions before any signature8 min▶

Run every proposal - vendor pitch, teacher request, board enthusiasm - through three questions, in order:

  • 1 · Guardrailed by design, or raw access? Does the product follow the Learning Mode / Khanmigo pattern - hints, questions, no direct answers - or does it hand students an answer machine? This is the a2 evidence as a purchase filter: same model, exam scores 17% down unguarded, practice up 127% with no exam harm when guardrailed.
  • 2 · Is there a data agreement? A written agreement that makes the vendor a "school official" under FERPA, keeps the institution in control, and bars reuse and training on student data. No agreement, no student accounts - that was a4's hard rail.
  • 3 · What is the under-13 posture? Consumer chatbot terms are 13+. Elementary use routes through teacher-mediated or walled-garden tools with parental consent handled properly - if the vendor has no answer here, they have not thought about your youngest students.
The question that is NOT on the rubric "Does it include an AI detector?" is a negative signal, not a feature. a3 covered the evidence; a6 gives you the protocol that replaces detection entirely.
LiveWhen "build" beats "buy"4 min▶

"Build" here rarely means software. The guardrailed-tutor pattern - Socratic questioning, hints, never direct answers - is a system prompt plus a policy, and the educator track constructs one hands-on in b6. Build is the right lane when:

  • The pedagogy is specific to you - your curriculum, your rubric language, your tier definitions. A benchmark product's guardrails are generic by necessity; a b6-style prompt encodes YOUR assessment design.
  • You already bought the platform - a campus-wide deal or a district workspace means the marginal cost of a purpose-built tutor is teacher time, not procurement.
  • A champion wants to own it - the maintenance question is real: prompts drift, models update, someone must re-test. No named owner, no build.

Either lane still runs on the same rails: the data agreement and under-13 posture questions apply to whatever platform hosts your build, exactly as they would to a purchase.

LiveThe corporate mirror: Bersin's maturity ladder for L&D leaders4 min▶

If you run corporate L&D rather than a school, the same roadmap logic maps onto Bersin's Corporate Learning Maturity Model - four levels: Static Training (roughly 33% of the market sits here) · Scaled Learning · Integrated Development · Dynamic Enablement. The stakes are not small: global corporate learning spend is about $400B, more than $1,400 per employee per year, and Level-4 organizations are 10x more likely to be innovation leaders.

The translation is direct. Course-catalog AI ("generate more modules faster") keeps you at Static Training with better wallpaper. The roadmap moves you up the same way a school's does: shared fluency first (the ladder), guardrailed tools in the flow of work (the rubric), and measurement on capability, not completions. b9 in the educator track works this mirror in detail.

Self-studyThe 12-month roadmap template, quarter by quarter4 min read▶

Fill this in for your own institution - one page, four quarters, each with an exit gate:

  • Q1 - Policy. Adopt the 3-tier policy pattern (a3), publish the privacy rails (a4), name the champions. Gate: policy signed and communicated before any tool decision.
  • Q2 - PD wave 1. Everyone climbs rungs 1-3 of the ladder; champions take rung 6. Gate: completions mapped onto the UNESCO grid, one artifact per teacher.
  • Q3 - Guardrailed pilots. One or two products that pass all three rubric questions, in a limited number of classes, with the data agreement signed first. Gate: pilot running with process evidence collected.
  • Q4 - Measure. Retention and learning outcomes, not engagement - the PNAS trap is that usage and satisfaction soar while learning falls. Add teacher hours saved. Gate: a board-ready readout that decides what scales.

a6 turns this skeleton into the full change-management playbook, including the one document you must write before Q3: the false-accusation protocol.
