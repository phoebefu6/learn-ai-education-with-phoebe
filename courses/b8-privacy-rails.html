The PII discipline + the three regimes 10 min live

The teacher's whole privacy practice fits in two rules. Rule 1, the 10-second habit: strip identity before you prompt. "Dana Okafor, IEP for written expression" becomes "a Grade 9 student who struggles with written organization" - same help, zero exposure. Rule 2: anything touching actual student work goes through vetted district tools ONLY. Rosters, grades, IEP or medical details, family situations - none of it ever enters a consumer chatbot. And this is not just good manners: ~12 US states say it explicitly - no student PII in AI systems, ever.

The habit is a rewrite, not a refusal - you still get the AI's help, the student just never enters the room:

The 10-second rewrite - same help, zero exposureA Grade 9 student who struggles with written organization is drafting a persuasive essay. Suggest three scaffolds I can offer.

The never-paste list, kept short on purpose: rosters, grades, IEP or medical details, family situations. If a prompt needs any of these to work, it needs a vetted district tool - or a rewrite.

The habit covers every input, not just typing A photo of a marked-up essay with a name in the header, a pasted email thread with a parent, a screenshot of the gradebook - each is a paste. Run the same 10-second check on anything that enters the prompt box, in any format. And deleting the chat afterwards fixes nothing: the disclosure already happened.
LiveFERPA in one card2 min▶

Student PII from education records entering a consumer AI tool is an unauthorized disclosure - unless the vendor qualifies as a "school official," and that exception has three teeth:

  • A written agreement between the vendor and the school - not a click-through ToS.
  • The school keeps control of the data, including what happens to it later.
  • No reuse for training - the data serves your students, never the vendor's model.

Your consumer chatbot account has none of the three. Practical rail: district-vetted tools with data protection agreements for anything touching student records; everything else gets the de-identified version.

LiveCOPPA in one card2 min▶

Under-13 use needs verifiable parental consent, and schools may consent only for educational use. But the sharper fact sits in the terms of service: consumer chatbot ToS are 13+ (OpenAI: 13+, and 13-18 with parental consent) - so an elementary class on a consumer chatbot is outside the vendor's own rules before COPPA even enters. The elementary pattern is teacher-mediated or walled-garden tools: the teacher drives, or the district tool holds the account.

LivePDPA + Singapore in one card2 min▶

Singapore shows what the guarded path looks like when a system builds it on purpose: MOE routes classroom AI through the Student Learning Space (SLS) with guardrails built in, and the AI Learning Assistant (LEA) runs from Primary 4, teacher-supervised in the early years. Under PDPA, student data is fully protected personal data and minors' consent runs through parents or guardians. The lesson travels: the safe route is the platform your system vetted, not the app in your pocket.

Part 3 · classroom safety beyond privacy

Age gates, parents, and vendor questions self-study

Privacy is the legal floor; safety is the classroom ceiling. Common Sense Education's safety-first framing is the useful lens here: assume young users will do the unexpected, and design so the unexpected is boring. Three practical pieces for after class.

Self-studyAge gates + teacher-mediated patterns for young classes3 min read▶

Age gates in practice: the ToS age is a hard floor, not a suggestion - 13+ for consumer chatbots, parental consent expectations for 13-18, and school consent only inside educational-use boundaries. For classes below the gate, two patterns do all the work:

  • Teacher-mediated - one screen, the teacher's hands on the keyboard, the class directing the prompts together. The students get the thinking practice; no student ever holds an account.
  • Walled garden - a district-vetted, education-built tool where the guardrails, logging, and data terms were negotiated for minors. Singapore's SLS + LEA is the national-scale version of exactly this.
Real world

Age terms sit on the teacher side too. Khanmigo - the Socratic, no-direct-answers tutor whose design DNA b6 borrowed - is free for educators 18 and over in 44+ countries (Microsoft-funded). Even purpose-built education tools publish an age posture; the vendor-questions card below turns that into a habit you run before any tool reaches your class.

Self-studyWhat to tell parents: the transparency letter3 min read▶

A one-page letter before the unit starts turns AI from a rumor into a policy. The template, as bullets:

  • What we use - the named, district-vetted tool, and what it is for in this unit.
  • What students do with it - the tier rules from b7, in one plain sentence each.
  • What never goes in - no names, no grades, no records; the de-identification habit, stated as a promise.
  • How work is assessed - portfolios, in-class anchors, and orals mean your child's own thinking is what gets graded.
  • Who to ask - you, with an email address, before it becomes a school-gate conversation.
Tone check Write it the way you would say it at pickup time: no acronyms, no defensiveness. Parents are not asking whether AI exists in your classroom - they are asking whether anyone is watching. The letter is the proof that someone is.
Self-studyFour questions before adopting any classroom tool3 min read▶

Ask the vendor - or the colleague recommending the shiny new app - these four, in order:

  • Is there a DPA? A signed data protection agreement with the district - the FERPA school-official test in paper form.
  • Is student data used for training? The only acceptable answer is a written no.
  • Can we delete? Deletion on request, with a stated timeline, for a named data owner at the school.
  • What is the under-13 posture? COPPA-compliant by design, teacher-mediated only, or honestly "not for under-13" - any of these beats a shrug.

A vendor who cannot answer all four in one email has answered anyway.

1 · The 10-second de-identification habit turns "Dana Okafor, IEP for written expression" into:
