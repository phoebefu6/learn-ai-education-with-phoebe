Part 1 · the full-course synthesis: every artifact from b3-b8, assembled

The Riverview unit, artifact by artifact 15 min live

Nothing tonight is new - that is the point. Eight artifacts, each built in an earlier session, snap together into one four-week unit. Walk the checklist in order; the order matters, because each artifact assumes the ones before it.

1 · The 3-tier policy statement (b7) - allowed / allowed only in certain scenarios / never allowed, printed on the unit brief and read aloud on day one. Everything else stands on this.

2 · The unit brief + objectives (b4) - "Should our city ban single-use plastics?", with objectives written so you can tell whether a student met them without the text as the only evidence.

3 · Scaffolded AI roles with not-do rules (b3) - which roles students may use at which stage, and what each role must NOT do (no thesis-writing, no full drafts).

4 · The guardrailed tutor prompt (b6) - hints, never answers, with the transcript-to-portfolio line built in: every tutor conversation lands in the portfolio automatically.

5 · Process portfolio checkpoints (b5) - outline, two drafts, AI transcript, in-class paragraph. The trail is the proof; skim on demand, like receipts.

6 · In-class anchor + oral defense schedule (b5) - one timed device-free paragraph as the baseline, and 28 five-minute orals booked into week 3 before anyone is suspicious of anything.

7 · The AI-transparent rubric row (b5) - "AI use disclosed, within tier, human thinking evident." Disclosure is graded, not confessed.

8 · The privacy pass (b8) - prompts de-identified, tools on the vetted list, under-13 rules checked (n/a for Grade 9, checked anyway - the habit is the artifact).

Print the checklist Eight lines on one sheet is the whole assembly. Pin it next to the b1 cheat sheet: the four levers were the diagnosis, these eight artifacts are the treatment, and every one of them maps to a lever - 1 to policy, 3-4 to roles, 5-7 to redesign, 8 to privacy - while 2, the brief, is the chassis the levers bolt onto.
LiveWhy the order matters4 min▶

Try assembling out of order and the machine jams - you saw every jam in the simulator across the course:

  • Portfolio before policy = evidence without a rulebook - retroactive rule-making (b1's Demo 1, rung 1).
  • Tutor before roles = a helpful bot with no not-do rules drifts back into an answer machine (b6's whole argument).
  • Orals only after suspicion = the oral becomes an interrogation instead of a scheduled, normal part of the unit (b5's design point).
  • Privacy pass last-minute = one un-vetted tool and the whole unit inherits an incident (b8).

Read aloud in week 1, introduced in week 2, defended in week 3, graded in week 4 - the unit teaches its own rules before it ever has to enforce them.

Real world

The assembled unit, on one page. Week 1: policy tiers read aloud + outline (portfolio starts). Week 2: draft 1 + the in-class anchor paragraph. Week 3: AI-assisted revision within tiers, transcript filed, 5-minute orals begin. Week 4: final draft + transparent-rubric grading. New teacher time: the orals (28 x 5 minutes, replacing one grading pass) and 90-second portfolio skims - only 5 of 28 ever needed a second look. Detector spend: zero.

Part 3 · your unit, your rollout, your staffroom

Now assemble YOUR unit 10 min live

In b5's homework you applied the four moves to one assignment you actually give. That artifact is your capstone seed. Tonight you run it through the same eight steps - policy statement, brief, roles, tutor prompt, portfolio checkpoints, anchor + orals, rubric row, privacy pass - and leave with a unit you can run next term.

LiveThe capstone assignment, formally4 min▶

One page per artifact, eight pages total, for the assignment you chose in b5:

  • 1 - your 3-tier policy statement, in student-readable language, one page.
  • 2 - the unit brief with objectives you can verify without the final text.
  • 3 - which AI roles are allowed at which stage, each with one not-do rule.
  • 4 - your tutor prompt, or the honest note "no tutor this unit" with the reason.
  • 5 - the portfolio checkpoints, with dates on your actual term calendar.
  • 6 - the anchor task + your three oral-defense questions.
  • 7 - your existing rubric with the one added AI row.
  • 8 - the privacy pass: which tools, which data rules, what never gets pasted.

Then score it against the 12 moments mentally: for each moment, ask "which of my eight pages handles this?" A moment with no page is a hole - and you know which session to reread.

LiveThe one-term rollout plan5 min▶

Do not launch all eight artifacts in one dramatic week. The rollout that works is the unit's own timeline, stretched over a term:

  • Week 1 - policy aloud. Read the 3-tier statement to the class. No tools yet, no drama - just one shared rulebook.
  • Week 2 - roles introduced. One scaffolded role at a time, each with its not-do rules, on low-stakes work first.
  • Week 3 - orals begin. Five minutes, three questions, scheduled for everyone - so the oral is normal before it is ever needed.
  • Week 4 - transparent-rubric grading. The AI row goes live once students have had three weeks to learn what disclosure looks like.

Each week is small enough to survive a bad day, and by week 4 the unit is enforcing itself.

LiveWhat to tell colleagues3 min▶

You will get two staffroom challenges. You already have both answers:

  • "Why not just buy a detector?" - the b5 line: "The detector's only unique contribution was accusing two honest students - everything real, the portfolio already caught." Then the numbers: 26% caught, 61% false flags on non-native writers, ~750 false accusations a year at Vanderbilt's scale.
  • "Isn't this a lot of extra work?" - the b7 framing: one policy read aloud beats a term of improvised rulings, and the orals replace a grading pass instead of adding one. Ms. Tan's portfolio skims ran 90 seconds each, and only 5 of 28 ever needed a second look.

Say both calmly, with the citations on this page open. You are not the teacher with an opinion - you are the teacher with the evidence.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · When does a redesigned unit ship?

The ship gate is behavioral, not documentary: the unit must survive all 12 classroom moments with the detector off. If it needs a detector to feel safe, a lever is missing - and the scorecard shows which one.

2 · The guardrailed tutor prompt, with its transcript-to-portfolio line - which session built it?

b3 scaffolds the roles, b5 builds the portfolio the transcripts land in - but the tutor prompt itself is b6's artifact, designed on the Bastani guardrail evidence: hints, never answers.

3 · The one-term rollout runs in which order?

Rules before tools, practice before stakes: the policy is read aloud before any role appears, orals become normal before they are ever needed, and grading with the AI row starts only after three weeks of learning what disclosure looks like.
